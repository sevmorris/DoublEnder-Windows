The first release of DoublEnder for Windows and Linux: the Local recorder from the Mac app, with the same faceplate and the same one-button flow. Pick a microphone and press the big button; the recording is saved to the Desktop, ready to send to the producer.

**Installing**
- **Windows:** download **DoublEnder-Setup.exe** and run it. It installs for your account only, with no administrator password, and puts DoublEnder in the Start menu and on the Desktop. The installer isn't signed yet, so Windows says it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**. Uninstall it from Settings > Apps; your recordings and settings stay.
- **Linux:** download **DoublEnder-linux-x86_64.tar.gz**, unpack it, and run `./install.sh` in the folder. DoublEnder is then in your applications menu.

**Recording**
- AAC (256 kbps M4A, the default, with the notes from Settings in the file) or 24-bit WAV, mono. An AAC take is encoded while it records, so STOP saves at once. The Windows installer brings its own encoder; on Linux, AAC needs ffmpeg installed, and without it DoublEnder records WAV.
- If the app or the computer crashes mid-take, the next launch offers to recover the recording as a WAV. The recovery file is the Mac app's format, so a take recovers on any of the three systems.
- A newly connected microphone brings up an offer to switch to it, except Bluetooth earbuds, whose microphones are call quality.

**For producers**
- DoublEnder writes a log for when something goes wrong: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\DoublEnder\DoublEnder.log` on Windows, `~/.local/state/DoublEnder/DoublEnder.log` on Linux. It holds device names and a line per take (dropped audio, disk stalls), never audio or notes.
- `DoublEnder-2.6.0x-windows-x64-symbols.zip` isn't needed to run DoublEnder. It reads a crash or hang dump from this exact build.
- `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the files' checksums.

Requires Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), or 64-bit Linux with glibc 2.39 or newer (Ubuntu 24.04 or later) and X11 or XWayland; on both, graphics with OpenGL 3.3.
